UNODC (in the field of counter trafficking) and GIZ, and more sporadically with CIVIPOL. Various examples were provided of joint activities or joint development of deliverables. For instance, country office IOM staff in Djibouti explained that “IOM has shared activities with GIZ where we [IOM] developed a budgeted roadmap for the implementation of the National Migration Strategy and GIZ has developed a monitoring and evaluation system to evaluate and to coordinate activities related to the roadmap.” Fewer examples were provided of synergies or shared activities with the British Council. The interview quotes below offer further examples of how activities were shared or differentiated between implementing partners and IOM:

“GIZ was able to establish local task forces in different districts. Therefore, when we went to the field, we could work with those focal persons that GIZ identified within the field to disseminate our message and for the messages and to be cascaded further. We also worked with GIZ on the development of the communication strategy and specific messages.”²⁴

“UNODC developed a curriculum in terms of trafficking in person for the National School of the Judiciary System, and from our part, we conducted a training for national stakeholders on the protection and identification of referral of victims of trafficking and vulnerable migrants. So UNODC was more focused on the persecution and judicial part, and we have mainly focused on the protection side.”²⁵

Nevertheless, the dual role of GIZ as both a “grant giver” and an implementer added complexity and occasionally created uncertainty among IOM regarding the division of labour or lines of communication. However, active engagement and cooperative relationship between the GIZ and IOM Regional Office helped to mitigate potential tensions and avoid overlaps.

Overall, IOM demonstrated strong internal and external coherence, with well-integrated activities across IOM programmes and effective collaboration with national and international partners. Joint implementation, cost-sharing, and alignment with regional migration frameworks enhanced the achievements of the programme.

### 3.3.1. Specific Objective 1: National and regional authorities in the field of migration governance are aware of, and act in accordance with, international and regional frameworks for migration governance and human rights standards. ... page 21

Specific Objective 1 of the BMM programme aims to enhance migration governance in the EHoA by aligning national and regional authorities with global and regional migration governance frameworks and human rights standards. This is to be achieved through a multi-faceted approach across the region and participating countries, encompassing building evidence bases for to support policy development, enhancing coordination between stakeholders through meetings, workshops and events, and conducting training and capacity building on key migration governance related concepts.
