### 3.3.2. Specific Objective 2: The quality of national and cross-border cooperation on trafficking and smuggling cases between law enforcement, judicial and other state and nonstate actors, in coordination with existing regional initiatives and in accordance with international obligations and standards, is increased ... page 26

Specific Objective 2 of the BMM programme aims to increase the quality of national and cross-border cooperation on trafficking and smuggling cases. This objective is to be achieved by aligning efforts with existing regional initiatives and adhering to international obligations and standards. The approach includes strengthening the capacity of state institutions, enhancing coordination between stakeholders through meetings, workshops, and events, and conducting training and capacity-building activities focused on key aspects of cross-border cooperation.

The evaluation found that IOM has enhanced national and cross-border cooperation on human trafficking and smuggling cases by supporting law enforcement agencies, judicial institutions, and civil society actors.

At the regional level, IOM achieved significant milestones by conducting two training sessions for frontline border officials and government officials in May and September 2023 on the operationalization of One Stop Border Posts (OSBPs), in collaboration with IOM's African Capacity Building Centre in Tanzania. These sessions utilized the newly published IBM Training Manual. Additionally, the IBM training manual was revised in French to be better utilized in Djibouti. In November 2023, IOM facilitated a regional IBM Training of Trainers (ToT) for border officials from Djibouti, Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, South Sudan, and Uganda, focusing on gender, human rights, and participatory development. This training led to improved understanding and capabilities in IBM principles, particularly in border operations and government authority identification. Additionally, in April 2024, IOM held a regional training on OSBP operations for participants from EAC and IGAD member states, covering topics such as balancing migration and human rights, trade facilitation, and ensuring gender mainstreaming and inclusion in border management.

At the country level, the evaluation found evidence of IOM activities contributing to the achievement of BMM's Specific Objective 2. These activities, as documented in IOM annual reports, include:

- In Djibouti, IOM assessed the Djibouti coastguard's equipment needs to enhance their response to migration-related challenges, including search and rescue operations. Following this assessment, IOM has provided essential search and rescue equipment materials to the Djibouti Coast Guard and Djiboutian Police, to support cross border management and migrant protection operations. Additionally, IOM and GIZ collaborated with Djibouti's Ministry of Interior and the Directorate General of the National Police to improve immigration services by providing equipment for processing E-Visa requests. This support followed a border post evaluation that identified infrastructure needs at key locations such as the Balho and Galilah Border Crossing Points. Finally, IOM supported two government officials to attend training on TiP and prosecution in Spain.

- In Ethiopia, IOM facilitated a study tour in Uganda for Ethiopian officials to learn best practices in IBM and address cross-border challenges. To further enhance capacity, IOM conducted two Integrated Border Governance (IBG) training sessions in April and August 2024, equipping 84 border officials with skills in migration management, gender issues, health at borders, and migrant protection.
