## 2.1. EVALUATION FRAMEWORK ... page 13

The evaluation used a theory-based approach, utilizing the intervention logic presented above to understand whether the implemented activities have indeed contributed to the envisaged outputs, outcomes, and impact. The Evaluation Matrix was developed based on the Organisation of Economic Cooperation and Development/Development Assistance Committee (OECD/DAC) evaluation criteria and quality standards. Besides the six criteria, the evaluation reviewed the cross-cutting issues of gender, human rights, peace and security, participatory development and good governance, poverty orientation and migration and climate change.

The evaluation matrix was updated from the Terms of Reference and based on initial conversations with IOM on the needs for the evaluation – and therefore the priorities for specific questions and criteria. Namely, the evaluation timeframe does not allow for comprehensive coverage of each question. The final report will cover all six evaluation criteria (Figure 2), but it will prioritise effectiveness, efficiency and sustainability, as agreed with IOM in the Inception Stage.

Each evaluation question includes the main data collection methods that will be used to collect information to answer the question, as well as key indicators that measure trends or the state of play/level regarding the change or situation the evaluation question refers to.

The main evaluation questions from the Terms of Reference (attached in Annex 1), are presented below, while the full evaluation matrix is added in Annex 2.

### Relevance: ... page 13

- Were IOM's interventions aligned with national and regional priorities, strategies and policies?
- Was the project relevant to the needs of beneficiaries and target groups? To what extent were those needs taken into account during project design?
- Did IOM's activities remain valid and pertinent as originally planned, or were they subsequently modified? (If modified, analysis on component with most modifications, reasons, and overview of bottlenecks)
- How did project stakeholders, including partners, beneficiaries, and duty-bearers, show their participation and ownership? More precisely, in what ways did they contribute to IOM's project design and implementation via collaboration, coordination, or providing support?
