At the regional level, IOM strengthened regional protection and awareness efforts through trainings (including Training of Trainers) on mental health and psychosocial support, gender-based violence, child protection, equipping frontline actors across multiple countries. It established an emergency fund, providing direct assistance to 549 migrants and returnees, comprising 267 adults and 282 children. The fund has provided various services, including interpretation support, voluntary return and reintegration, facilitation of consular and travel documentation, land and air transportation, food and non-food items, accommodation, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and medical care across the BMM countries: Kenya, Uganda, Somalia, Djibouti, and Ethiopia. IOM marked World Day Against Trafficking with awareness campaigns reaching millions through media and community events. For instance, in November 2023, IOM held a three-day awareness raising workshop with participants from CSOs and government agencies in Kenya, Uganda, and Somalia. Additionally, IOM conducted a functional review of the Migration Response Centre Information System to improve migrant service delivery across four such centres: in Bossaso (Somalia), Obock (Djibouti), Hargeisa (Somalia), and Kassala (Sudan). Additionally, IOM conducted a training on the collection, analysis, and use of data for disability inclusion, focusing on its application in protection programming within the migration context.

At the country level, the following IOM key activities, in line with SO3, were documented:

- In Djibouti, IOM conducted trainings for CSOs and frontline social workers to enhance protection services for vulnerable migrants, including updating the National Referral Mechanism. Through the Djibouti Red Crescent and the IOM transit centres and satellite offices IOM also provided direct assistance to 12,030 migrants (8456 male, 1593 female), covering food, water, medical aid, hygiene, and psychosocial support.
- In Ethiopia, IOM promoted community engagement to address irregular migration through peer education training and the establishment of Community Conversation Groups in high-migration areas. Trainings focused on protection, law enforcement cooperation, and support for trafficking victims. IOM also facilitated the validation of a victim hotline handbook for frontline protection actors, which will be translated into Amharic, printed, and followed by a ToT and workshop. IOM is supporting the hotline's operationalization at the Ministry of Justice. Additionally, IOM strengthened capacity at the Tog Wajaale Migrant Resource Center and trained staff (a total of 48 officials) in Metema and Tog Wajaale on migrant protection. In line with the recommendations of the first Joint Border Administrators and Governors Meeting, the first Ethiopia-South Sudan Joint Border Trade Committee Meeting was held in March 2025 in, Ethiopia. The meeting aimed to review the implementation status of the Border Trade Protocol and identify ways to address challenges in its execution. Finally, IOM is also developing an NRM training package, with its validation conducted in March 2025.
- In Kenya, IOM strengthened protection and counter-trafficking measures through trainings, several awareness campaigns, and policy development. Over 500 children and community members participated in World Day Against Trafficking events. IOM piloted community-led anti-trafficking initiatives, engaged migrant advocates, and enhanced NRM implementation. Also, IOM provided remediation guidelines for domestic migrant workers in Kenya, with the aim of offering support and redress for workers who have faced exploitation.
- In Somalia, IOM focused more on "leveraging internal expertise" by holding internal consultations and conducting Somali stakeholder mapping or conducting baseline surveys to roll out awareness
