## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS ... page 44

Following the findings and conclusions of the evaluation, the following recommendations have been developed to foster learning enhance the effectiveness, efficiency, and sustainability of IOM's interventions under future phases of the BMM Programme and beyond.

**Recommendation 1**: Maintain a strong internal coordination structure and retain qualified staff to ensure efficiency and effective implementation of activities under BMM Phase IV

The current management structure has proven essential in overseeing multi-country implementation, enabling responsiveness to evolving contexts, and leveraging the expertise of regional and country-level managers. Given BMM IV's core objective of strengthening regional cooperation, strategic management from the Regional Office is essential. This positioning enables IOM to engage more effectively with key regional organizations and processes such as IGAD, the African Union, ECA, and the Khartoum Process, ensuring alignment with broader regional migration governance frameworks. In addition, the Regional Office is uniquely placed to coordinate cross-border and regional initiatives, foster synergies across participating countries, and ensure the inclusion of Uganda (which will no longer be an independent participating country under BMM IV) through regional engagement and activities that transcend national boundaries. Hence, to sustain achieved outcomes and ensure efficiency and effectiveness during BMM Phase IV, the evaluation team recommends to:

- Keep the Regional Office as the primary coordination and management hub for BMM Phase IV, while maintaining focal points in each BMM country to ensure localized responsiveness and effective implementation.

**Recommendation 2**: Enhance internal cross-country cooperation and synergies with implementing partners

Internal coordination can be further strengthened by enhancing communication between country offices:

- Strengthen information-sharing and cooperation across different country teams, for instance, by having monthly or quarterly online meetings with all country teams and Regional Office.

Externally, IOM and partners could move beyond information-sharing meetings to more strategic planning sessions, as coordinated by GIZ the lead implementing partner, also ensuring cross-country cooperation:

- Some stakeholders noted that monthly coordination meetings chaired by GIZ focused primarily on updates rather than substantive collaboration. To optimize coordination (when needed), these meetings could incorporate **action-oriented discussions** on joint activities (such as multi-agency simulations and joint training programs), geographic alignment of activities, and opportunities for shared resources.

- Additionally, smaller, thematic working groups among implementing partners could be introduced to allow for more targeted collaboration and problem-solving (when needed). This can be proposed on an ad-hoc basis.

- Cross-country exchanges could be organized more frequently to facilitate knowledge-sharing and best practices.
