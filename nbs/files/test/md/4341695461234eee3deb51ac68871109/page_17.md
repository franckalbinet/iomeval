## 3.1.1. Alignment with regional and national needs ... page 17

IOM’s activities under BMM Phase III were well-aligned with the migration challenges and priorities of the region, demonstrating strong relevance at both national and regional levels.

Migration within the East and Horn of Africa region, and from the region towards the Middle East and Europe, is highly risky, particularly for refugees, displaced populations, and irregular migrants. The migration flow along the Eastern Route to the Arabian Peninsula has traditionally been one of the world’s busiest and continuously growing maritime migration routes. This route sees both outward migration and return movements, including voluntary and forced returns. Against this backdrop, the shared migration issues have to be addressed through a cross-country, regional approach as “tackling this problem only at a national level would simply risk displacing the existing smuggling/trafficking route”.¹⁵ Interviewed IOM staff confirmed that, while countries have their own unique contexts, they face similar challenges related to migration, warranting a joint and coordinated regional response.¹⁶

Migration governance in the region is affected by weak institutions, inconsistent legal and policy frameworks across the region, and high levels of corruption which all hinder effective national and cross-border cooperation on trafficking and irregular migration.¹⁷ A needs assessment conducted for the design of the EUTF in 2015 found that countries part of the Khartoum Process share the need for increased capacity (e.g. related to border management and data collection), increased awareness among the general population, and protection of migrants.¹⁸ Interviewees mentioned various challenges faced by migrants in the region, ranging from the consequences of trafficking and smuggling to the absence of awareness of human rights among migrants. For example, interviewed IOM staff noted the complicated nature of protection of victims: “hotels are not always safe enough for migrants fleeing from organised crime. We need safe houses”.¹⁹ Children form a particularly vulnerable group and are also prone to fall victim to recruitment by trafficking and smuggling rings.²⁰
