Similarly, Somalia's accession to the East African Community (EAC) accelerated the need for harmonized border management, prompting the programme to fast-track the establishment of Integrated border management committees.

The programme also demonstrated responsiveness to government requests. One IOM interviewee in Djibouti described a case in which a national government shifted priorities midway through the programme: “We agreed to provide support to the expansion of the MIDAS. However, in the middle of the project, the government decided that they did not want this system anymore. We reallocated the budget for MIDAS to support the government with their e-visa system instead.” Another example of adaptive management practices was given by IOM country office staff in Kenya: “Thanks to the flexibility and the regular discussions on the work plan, we were able to make sure that BMM was relevant throughout Phase III. There was a request from the Government for consultations on an integrated border management policy. This was not something that was part of BMM from the beginning, but we were able to move resources around to respond to the request.”

National contexts evolved differently during the implementation of Phase III, with different effects on the implementation of the programme. Interviewed IOM staff confirmed that they aimed to adjust to unforeseen circumstances or changing contexts as much as possible within the BMM framework. This flexibility was enabled by the design of the overall BMM programme, which allowed country office IOM staff to agree with national stakeholders on priorities and objectives for the national level, instead of “imposing a set of activities”. This ensured that the regional BMM Theory of Change was relevant for each country and aligned with (changing) priorities.²³

## 3.2. COHERENCE ... page 19

The coherence of IOM’s activities under BMM Phase III is assessed in relation to their alignment with other IOM initiatives in the region, as well as with the work of other implementing partners. This analysis considers both internal coordination within IOM and external coherence with national governments, regional bodies, and other international organizations.

### 3.2.1. Internal alignment with other efforts of IOM ... page 19

IOM activities under BMM Phase III demonstrated strong internal coherence by leveraging synergies with other IOM projects, enabling cost-sharing and expanding programme reach.

The number of ongoing IOM projects and initiatives differed across the six programme countries. A key strategy for internal coherence was the co-funding and joint implementation of activities, allowing for more comprehensive migration governance interventions. In many cases, such synergies focused on joint activities and cost-sharing (as mentioned under efficiency) but also on expanding the scope of activities to more geographical areas of the country.
