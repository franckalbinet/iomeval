

## 1.1 REGIONAL MIGRATION CONTEXT … page 8

Migration in the East and Horn of Africa (EHoA) is shaped by a complex
mix of socio-economic, political, and environmental factors. According
to the state of migration report for 2022, the region hosts
approximately 8.5 million international migrants, with a significant
majority (64%) being refugees and asylum seekers. In total, 22.3 million
individuals in the region are displaced, including 16.9 million
internally displaced persons. Forced displacement remains the
predominant driver of migration, particularly in countries such as South
Sudan, Uganda, Ethiopia, Somalia, Eritrea, and Kenya, which
simultaneously serve as both sources and hosts of refugees. The scale of
displacement is fuelled by persistent conflicts, governance challenges,
and climate-related disasters, making Eastern Africa one of the most
migration-affected regions globally.

In addition to forced displacement, both regular and irregular labour
migration play significant roles in shaping migration patterns. However,
irregular migration is particularly prevalent due to the presence of
well-established smuggling and trafficking networks, which take
advantage of weak border controls and governance structures.² These
criminal networks not only exploit vulnerable migrants but also
contribute to corruption within government institutions, undermining
migration management efforts.³ Furthermore, the region’s exposure to
climate shocks has exacerbated food insecurity and displacement, further
complicating migration governance. For example, in early 2023, the
region experienced a prolonged drought (the worst in over four decades)
which resulted in over 2 million people being internally displaced
within Ethiopia and Somalia.⁴

Recognizing these challenges, regional cooperation frameworks have been
established to facilitate more effective migration management. The
Khartoum Process, launched in 2014, serves as a key platform for
political dialogue and collaboration between countries along migration
routes from the Horn of Africa to Europe. It aims to enhance cooperation
on migration and mobility, implement projects targeting human
trafficking and smuggling, and strengthen regional partnerships between
countries of origin, transit, and destination. Since its inception, the
Khartoum Process has underpinned several initiatives, including the BMM
programme, which has contributed to improved migration governance,
border management, and protection mechanisms across the region. Despite
these efforts, continued political commitment and sustained regional
cooperation remain crucial to addressing the root causes of migration
and ensuring safer, more orderly, and regular migration pathways.
