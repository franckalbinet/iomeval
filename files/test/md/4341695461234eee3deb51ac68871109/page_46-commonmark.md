**Recommendation 5**: Strengthen national ownership, commitment, and
capacities

To ensure the sustainability of migration governance efforts, national
governments should increase their ownership and commitment to key
programme activities. This could include both financial commitments and
support with personnel. Many countries remain heavily dependent on
external funding, which risks programmatic disruptions when donor
support ends. IOM (together with other implementing partners) could:

- Encourage participating states to take greater ownership of the
  programme’s activities by introducing a co-funding principle where
  governments commit to gradually take over financial responsibilities
  after an initial donor-funded period.

- In terms of communication with national stakeholders, some
  interviewees suggested that formal communication channels i.e. through
  official letters could be strengthened to ensure timely and clear
  communication of changes and updates, potentially reducing delays in
  implementation.

- Develop transition plans and exit strategies to outline clear steps
  for continued policy implementation beyond donor-supported programmes.

In addition, capacity-building initiatives under BMM Phase III have
proven effective in strengthening national migration institutions. To
ensure long-term sustainability:

- Trainings could be embedded within national institutions such as law
  enforcement and standardized training programs could be developed to
  institutionalize knowledge transfer.

- The Training of Trainers approach could be expanded to equip
  stakeholders with the necessary tools and knowledge to effectively
  pass on the training to others.

- Mentorship and peer-learning opportunities, such as the Ethiopia-Kenya
  study tour on border management, could be expanded to foster
  cross-country learning.
