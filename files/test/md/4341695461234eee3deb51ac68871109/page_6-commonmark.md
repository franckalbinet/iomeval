<td></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Did IOM deliver what it was supposed to do?</td>
<td>IOM met or exceeded most of its key performance indicators under the
BMM Phase III results framework, particularly in enhancing migration
governance, strengthening institutional capacities, and improving victim
protection. Some outputs, such as training border officials and
supporting policy development, surpassed initial targets.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>What hindered the implementation of the programme and achievement of
outcomes?</td>
<td>Several factors hindered implementation, including security
challenges (e.g., armed conflicts and political instability in Ethiopia,
and Somalia), delayed signing of the contractual agreement between IOM
and GIZ, procurement bottlenecks, and changing government priorities
that affected planned activities. Additionally, limited financial and
human resources in some IOM country offices slowed down
implementation.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Efficiency</td>
<td></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>To what extent were IOM activities financially efficient?</td>
<td>While IOM successfully leveraged cost-sharing and maximized
available resources, delayed contract signing at the start of Phase III
led to low burn rates initially and a rush to spend remaining funds
toward the end. Despite this, no major inefficiencies in resource
allocation were identified.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>To what extent was IOM efficient in terms of coordination and
reporting?</td>
<td>Internal coordination was strong, with IOM’s Regional Office for the
East, Horn and Southern Africa as the management site, organising
regular meetings between regional and country offices. Reporting
mechanisms were well-structured and described as straightforward and
manageable by staff despite frequent reporting requirements from the
donor.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Externally, some joint activities were conducted with other
implementing partners, but further integration could be explored by GIZ
as lead implementer to align partners’ implementation timelines and
create further synergies.</td>

<tr>
<td>What initial signs of impact of IOM activities can be noted?</td>
<td>IOM’s activities have contributed to strengthened migration
governance, with progress in policy development in several countries.
Cross-border cooperation mechanisms, such as Inter-Agency Border
Management Committees (IBMCs) in Somalia, have improved operational
coordination. Additionally, community awareness campaigns and protection
services have enhanced support for vulnerable migrants, though long-term
impacts remain difficult to measure at this stage.</td>
</tr>

<td>Sustainability</td>
<td></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>To what extent the achievements of IOM activities are likely to be
sustained long-term?</td>
<td>While IOM has helped institutionalize migration management through
policies, training, and capacity-building, sustainability remains a
concern. Many governments still rely on external funding, and gaps in
national budgetary commitments threaten long-term program viability. The
expected funding gap between BMM Phase III and IV could also disrupt
continuity, potentially leading to staff turnover and loss of
institutional knowledge.</td>
