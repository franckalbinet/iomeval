tangible improvements in national migration policies and institutional
frameworks. Countries such as Ethiopia, Kenya, and Somalia have
developed or refined policies aligned with international standards
(e.g., the Global Compact for Migration), fostering a more structured
approach to migration management. These policies, developed with the
support of IOM, are likely to create “spill-over” effects leading to
further development of legislative acts on migration. Highlighting the
IOM’s role in shaping migration governance, a national government
interviewee explained: “the National Migration Policy framework… will be
the document that guides the formulation of other policies in \[the
country\]”.

Importantly, IOM activities have strengthened government commitment to
addressing migration challenges, as one national stakeholder noted: “The
new directives and laws have strengthened the government’s commitment to
regular migration and improved coordination among relevant actors.”
Another national government representative emphasized: “The biggest win
for the program… is that you can see that the topic of migration
management is really being seen at a high level.”

Second, some of the IOM activities strengthened cross-border
cooperation, for instance, through initiatives like the establishment of
IBMCs and the implementation of the MIDAS. These efforts have
contributed to a better coordination and efficiency of border
management, reducing the risks associated with irregular migration and
enhancing the security and protection of migrants.

Finally, IOM has contributed to the protection of vulnerable migrants,
for instance, through the support to migrant response centres,
capacity-building initiatives, the development of NRMs, and the
provision of direct assistance. The latter was very much appreciated, as
one government stakeholder emphasized: “IOM is very good in direct
assistance to migrants. They have migrant response centres and other
tools.”

The programme’s awareness-raising campaigns and community engagement
initiatives have played a crucial role in potentially changing attitudes
and behaviors towards migration. Activities like the Community
Conversation Facilitators Training in Ethiopia and the Migrants as
Messengers initiative in Kenya have empowered communities to address
migration challenges proactively. These efforts have increased awareness
of the risks associated with irregular migration and the available
support mechanisms, contributing to safer migration practices. Training
sessions, for instance, on MHPSS, GBV, risk mitigation, and child
protection have strengthened frontline workers’ skills needed to support
and protect migrants effectively. One national partner noted, however,
that longer-term assignments are needed for awareness-raising campaigns
(more than three-four months) to have larger impact.

Overall, IOM has laid the groundwork for long-term improvements in
migration and border governance, although, as outlined in the next
section, there are concerns about the sustainability of these
achievements without continued support.
