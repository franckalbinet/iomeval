  procurement processes” caused delays in procuring digital screens for
  awareness campaigns (IOM interim report 2024). IOM addressed this by
  conducting a mapping exercise to determine needs and streamline the
  procurement process.
- In Djibouti, the E-Visa system and coastguard equipment procurement
  were delayed, requiring IOM to conduct assessments, identify priority
  border points, and adjust procurement timelines.
- In Somalia, “delays in codifying the immigration regulations into law
  and the reconvening of Parliament from recess, necessitated
  adjustments in the rollout timelines for the Immigration Act and
  related training sessions, complicating the planning and
  implementation of these activities” (IOM interim report 2024).

A general perception among national respondents was that “their
\[IOM’s\] procurement is very slow”. Although some of the delays related
to factors external to IOM, they pose a reputational risk for IOM. In
Somalia, for instance, the mentioned delays in trainings were perceived
by one government respondent as an issue of IOM’s “internal procurement
process”³⁴ – even if the actual reasons for delay might have been more
complex. Similarly, a government respondent recalled delays in the pay
out of Daily Subsistence Allowances (DSA) to participants of a training
that the government co-organized: “There was a time when we brought
people for training. We brought them from the regions, and we put them
in hotels… Once the time came when their DSA was supposed to be paid,
there were really delays. It fell on a weekend, \[IOM\] said… This made
us look bad in front of the people who travelled from the regions \[and
did not get reimbursed on time\].”

### Enabling factors – adaptive management, regular stakeholder engagement, and cordial relationships … page 34

Key enabling factors included regular stakeholder engagement, adaptive
planning and management, and securing additional funding sources, which
showcased IOM’s resilience and flexibility, allowing progress despite
challenging operating environments. Consider, for instance, the National
Partnership Coalition assessments in Ethiopia which were initially
scheduled for three regions. However, due to budget constraints and
logistical challenges, the focus was narrowed to two regions: Oromia and
Amhara. While the scope of the activity was narrowed down, it was done
strategically considering these regions’ proximity to Addis Ababa,
allowing for more efficient use of resources and better coordination.
Furthermore, several interviewees referred to the importance of strong
professional relationships which have been established over the years
between the different partner entities and organizations. Such cordial
relationships allowed navigating above-mentioned challenges more
efficiently, thereby contributing to the achievement of programme’s
objectives.

For instance, although one civil society organization experienced issues
due to procurement delays on the IOM’s side (transition to the new WAVE
system), the national partner was briefed and informed and thus showed
understanding and overall satisfaction with the relationship:

“We have had some instances where the procurement process took some
time… but of course we ironed out that out with them and they promised
to improve… The cooperation overall was fine… it has been great
cooperating with them \[IOM\], working with them and they are trying to
do their best.”

Overall, IOM’s ability to navigate security risks, coordination
challenges, financial constraints, procurement delays, and policy
bottlenecks was crucial for ensuring the continued implementation of BMM
activities.

³⁴ Interview with a national government respondent.
