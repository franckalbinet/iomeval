

## 1.2 BETTER MIGRATION MANAGEMENT (BMM) PROGRAMME … page 9

### 1.2.1 Design of the BMM programme … page 9

In 2016, the German Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and
Development and the European Union, specifically the EU’s Trust Fund for
Africa (EUTF)⁵, jointly established the Better Migration Management
(BMM) Programme to respond to the needs identified by the African member
states of the Khartoum Process. The overall objective of BMM was to
“Enable national authorities and institutions to facilitate safe,
orderly, and regular migration, and effectively address and reduce
trafficking in human beings and the smuggling of migrants within and
from the Horn of Africa region by applying a human rights-based
approach.”⁶

The programme had three components to achieve its objective:

1.  Migration governance to establish a political basis for regionally
    harmonized migration management
2.  Effective institutions to address and reduce trafficking in human
    beings and the smuggling of migrants
3.  Protection of victims of trafficking and vulnerable migrants.

In 2022, BMM III was launched and the programme is implemented by five
partner organisations: the International Organization for Migration
(IOM), the British Council, CIVIPOL⁷, the United Nations Office on Drugs
and Crime (UNODC) and the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale
Zusammenarbeit (GIZ). GIZ is furthermore the lead implementing partner,
and IOM’s direct funder. The main stakeholders of the programme are
government authorities which includes law enforcement, border officials,
judiciary, women/social affairs, counter trafficking in persons and
national coordination for migration mechanisms; civil society (primarily
protection actors); Victims of Trafficking (VoTs) and vulnerable
migrants; and communities which are migrant hot spots.⁸

Between 2016 and 2022, BMM’s Phase I and Phase II were implemented in
Djibouti, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, and
Uganda. Alignment with existing mechanisms and initiatives of the
African Union Commission and the Intergovernmental Authority on
Development (IGAD) was key to the programme.⁹

### 1.2.2 Design and objectives of the BMM Phase III … page 9

The third phase of the BMM continued activities under the same objective
and three components of implementation as the previous phases. BMM
newsletters emphasize, however, that Phase III has also focused on how
to make the impact of the overall BMM programme sustainable: “The third
phase will build on the previous ones and focuses on consolidating a
holistic regional approach to migration management and ensuring its
sustainability after the programme has ended.”¹⁰
