    The evaluation found that IOM has made substantial progress in improving migration governance frameworks in the EHoA region. The programme successfully engaged with national and regional authorities to harmonize migration policies with international standards and strengthen institutional capacities.

    At the regional level, IOM supported policy and coordination mechanisms. In September 2023, BMM countries and an East African Community (EAC) representative attended a workshop in Entebbe on mixed migration flows and the migration-environment-climate nexus. In November 2024, IOM supported the first EAC Regional Consultative Process (RCP) on Migration, bringing together various EAC member states²⁶. A key outcome was the creation of the EAC RCP’s Action Plan on Migration. In July 2023, IOM held a virtual meeting to introduce a report on the mapping of migration policies in the EHoA produced under BMM III, with the African Union Commission, IGAD, and EAC. This helped stakeholders understand regional migration policy advancements. In May 2024, IOM hosted a workshop on Digital Legal Identity for 31 government officials from nine African countries. The workshop covered digital ID systems, data protection, and legal frameworks, aligning with Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) 16.9 and 17.19.2, contributing to the Common and Free Movement Protocols of the EAC and IGAD.

    At the country level, the evaluation found evidence for IOM activities contributing to the achievement of BMM’s Specific Objective 1. Some of these activities, as documented in IOM annual reports, include:

    • In Djibouti, IOM partnered with the National Coordination office for Migration in Djibouti (BCNM) Office to conduct workshops presenting the national migration strategy and its five-year action plan. Additionally, IOM supported the finalization of a budgeted roadmap for the National Migration Strategy and its five-year action plan through consultations. Capacity-building for the BCNM included office equipment and training on migration governance, the Global Compact for Migration (GCM), and SDGs. IOM supported Institute National de la Statistique et de la Démographie to conduct its population census, develop a report on migrant information from the census, and to develop a National Statistical Yearbook on Migration and Displacement. Furthermore, in May 2024, a study tour to Tanzania provided Djiboutian stakeholders insights into border management and interagency cooperation. In July 2024, IOM trained participants on labor migration governance and bilateral agreements.

    • In Ethiopia, IOM initiated the second phase of the Organizational Capacity Assessment (OCA) to assist National and Regional Partnership Coalitions (NPC and RPC) in evaluating their strengths and challenges, which was compiled into an Organisational Development Plan with corresponding capacity building initiatives. IOM facilitated the NPC’s biannual review meeting and participated in multiple strategic planning discussions, including the implementation of the five-year Strategic Plan and migration reviews at national and regional levels. Moreover, IOM continued advocacy efforts for the endorsement of Ethiopia’s National Migration Policy, alongside support for key review workshops, including the Oromia RPC annual review and Dire Dawa RPC Secretariat’s planning sessions. Additionally, Capacity-building activities included the Essentials of Migration Management training, an M&E workshop, and an OCA presentation on the NPC and RCP results.

    ²⁶ Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, South Sudan, Rwanda, Burundi, the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), and Somalia.
