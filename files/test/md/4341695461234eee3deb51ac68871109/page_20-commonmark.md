IOM efforts to ensure internal coherence:

“We were planning to conduct an assessment on border management and
management of mixed migration flows. Under BMM, we would be able to
focus on one border. However, we had some resources under another
project which we could use to expand the assessment to all borders.”

“The BMM project was able to support the implementation of the NCM
\[National Coordination Mechanism\]. However, through the support of
another project, we were also able to establish sub-national NCMs in
major cities. This allowed us to get the full picture on migration
governance and its indicators, from the grassroots to the to the
national level.”

This strategic alignment allowed IOM to optimize resources and avoid
duplication of efforts, ensuring a broader and more impactful reach. One
of the key enabling factors to ensure internal coherence mentioned by
the interview partners was the IOM Regional Office’s active role in
maintaining a broader perspective and ensuring cross-country synergies
and coordination and alignment to regional frameworks. For instance, the
Regional Office made sure that IOM activities under BMM at the same time
contribute to the Regional Migrant Response Plan coordinated by the IOM.
Overall, no significant overlaps or duplications between IOM activities
under BMM programme and other IOM initiatives were flagged to the
evaluation team by the interviewees.

### 3.2.2. External alignment with efforts and organizations outside IOM … page 20

IOM’s activities under BMM Phase III also demonstrated strong external
coherence, successfully aligning with regional migration frameworks,
national policies, and international donor-funded initiatives. IOM
worked alongside BMM implementing partners UNODC, GIZ, CIVIPOL, and the
British Council. However, while some collaborations were
well-integrated, others remained limited, with partners working in silos
rather than through a fully coordinated approach.

BMM Phase III demonstrated strong coherence with other international and
national migration-related programmes, often integrating activities with
those of the European Union, bilateral donors, and regional
organizations. This was particularly evident in areas such as migrant
protection, governance, and border management. For example, in Djibouti,
through BMM, IOM partnered with the Djibouti Red Crescent to provide
direct assistance for transitory and stranded migrants. In Uganda, BMM
and the Swiss-funded Spotlight Initiative collaborated on addressing
gender-based violence in migration contexts.

Additionally, BMM Phase III aligned with regional frameworks such as
IGAD Regional Migration Policy Framework, promoting cross-border
cooperation and policy harmonization. This regional approach was
critical, as tackling migration challenges at a national level alone
risked shifting smuggling and trafficking routes rather than addressing
root causes. A stakeholder emphasized this point: “The BMM project was
instrumental in bringing the governments in this region together so that
they can actually informally have a dialogue on issues of migration
governance.”
