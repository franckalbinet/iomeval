IOM staff explained this referring to contractual delays at the
beginning of Phase III (signing the contract between IOM and GIZ),
national stakeholder engagement as well as limited resources of IOM
country offices: “IOM is operating in very, very vulnerable places. At
the missions’ level, the capacities are very low. In combination with
slow responses from the governments, this creates delays”.

To be sure, the evaluation team did not find evidence for negative
consequences of such budgetary dynamics. Nevertheless, the need to
rapidly increase the burn rate towards the end of a phase naturally
creates risks for strategic, coordinated planning and implementation.

By the time of data collection and analysis (February 2025), IOM staff
across the six countries reported that activities had either been
completed or were almost completed. Most IOM BMM focal points also
reported that the budget will be used entirely by the end of the Phase
III (March 2025).

Although more can always be done with more funding, interviewed IOM
staff were generally satisfied with the allocation of financial
resources, as they were aligned with the planned activities.\[38\] Some
examples were provided of top-ups received during Phase III, e.g. for
the response to returnees, refugees or Third Country Nationals from
Sudan. Furthermore, different IOM offices provided examples of
cost-sharing, either through shared activities with other IOM projects,
or with other BMM partners.\[39\]

“We were organising an activity in the region for BMM. Because we were
able to cost-share the activity with another IOM project, we were able
to gather many more participants and therefore make the activity more
impactful than if we had only used the BMM budget. Of course, the costs
were still reported separately to avoid conflicts with the donors”.40
