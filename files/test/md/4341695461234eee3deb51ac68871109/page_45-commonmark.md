**Recommendation 3: Ensure continuity between BMM programme phases**

One of the risks identified in the evaluation is the gap between the end
of BMM Phase III and the start of Phase IV, which could lead to staffing
disruptions and loss of institutional memory and programmatic momentum.
To mitigate this:

- Transition plans should be developed to maintain core programme
  personnel and ensure continuity in operations. Additionally, donors
  should consider allowing carry-over of funds and approving no-cost
  extensions to ensure continuity, while also committing to multi-year
  funding to enhance predictability and stability in program delivery.
  Lessons from previous transitions between BMM phases indicate that
  minimizing staff turnover is essential for maintaining established
  partnerships and ensuring smooth implementation.
- If possible, GIZ and implementing partners should align their
  implementation timelines under Phase IV to avoid missed opportunities
  for joint actions and enhance sustainability.
- Based on Phase II and III experience, IOM should anticipate
  contractual delays and changing national priorities during BMM Phase
  IV and plan its budgetary spending accordingly, to prevent last-minute
  spending pressures and risks of unspent funds at the end of the
  programme.

**Recommendation 4: Strengthen procurement and financial coordination to
streamline processes and prevent delays**

To mitigate procurement-related delays and enhance the efficiency of
project implementation, IOM should adopt a proactive and structured
approach to procurement planning and coordination. While some delays
stem from external factors, improving internal processes and
coordination mechanisms can help reduce bottlenecks and ensure smoother
implementation. The following actions are recommended:

- Clarify roles and responsibilities within IOM at regional and mission
  level for procurement related submissions and approvals that are not
  reflected in IOM procurement guidelines.
- Develop and share a procurement plan at project inception to enhance
  coordination and foresight. This will ensure early awareness of
  upcoming procurement needs and facilitate timely processing.
- During the contracting phase with GIZ and other partners, clearly
  define and agree on any alternative procurement procedures to
  streamline decision-making and avoid delays due to procedural
  mismatches.
- To the extent possible, procurement requests should be initiated early
  in the project cycle, particularly for items known to have longer
  processing times, to prevent last-minute disruptions.
- To manage expectations and maintain trust, IOM should proactively
  communicate procurement timelines and potential delays with government
  stakeholders and implementing partners. This will help address
  concerns over perceived inefficiencies and safeguard IOM’s reputation.
