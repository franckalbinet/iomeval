

MIDAS Training of Trainers took place from December 2-6, with 23 border
officials from Bole International Airport. Additionally, IOM, with Swiss
government co-funding, installed the MIDAS at Addis Ababa Bole
International Airport, processing almost 130,000 visas on arrival since
its launch in May 2024. In October 2024, ICS assessed three Border
Crossing Points (BCPs) – Galafi, Tog Wajaale, and Kurmuk. Looking ahead,
IOM plans to enhance MIDAS by adding a module in conjunction with the
Ministry of Women and Social Affairs that supports the identification
and referral of vulnerable migrants upon their return.

- In Kenya, IOM has strengthened migration management through
  capacity-building initiatives, policy assessments, and community
  engagement. IOM sponsored 15 students for a postgraduate diploma in
  migration management at the Kenya Institute of Migration Studies and
  supported 19 border officials through a training program. IOM
  facilitated three mobile registration drives in Kajiado, Kisumu, and
  Kwale counties, enabling 6,586 individuals to obtain legal documents.
  In May 2024, IOM trained 22 law enforcement officials in Turkana on
  migration management and TiP prevention, followed by community
  workshops reaching 650 participants. IOM also conducted a Humanitarian
  Border Management assessment in collaboration with the Border
  Management Secretariat, engaging 117 officials to identify challenges
  and recommend improvements. IOM also donated communication equipment
  to Secretariat, co-hosted the first National Border Management
  Conference, and organized a study visit to the UK for 10 border
  management officials, providing insights into security risk management
  and advanced screening techniques.

- In Somalia, IOM has enhanced migration management through
  institutional capacity building, policy implementation support, and
  cross-border cooperation. IOM facilitated the establishment of five
  IBMCs to strengthen coordination between Points of Entry and trained
  new IBMC members in different locations. To combat Trafficking in
  Persons, IOM trained frontline officers on victim identification,
  international migration law, and human rights standards. A study tour
  to Rwanda enabled Somali officials to observe best practices in
  inter-agency border operations. IOM also supported the rollout of the
  Immigration Act and ICA Law by training 42 officers from key entry
  points, including Mogadishu’s airport, seaport, and enrolment centres.
  The training provided practical guidance on implementing new policies.
  Additionally, IOM facilitated multiple trainings on Standard Operating
  Procedures (SOPs) for IBMCs across different regions, reinforcing
  Somalia’s border management framework.

- In South Sudan, IOM organized seven trainings for 169 border officials
  (127 male, 42 female), held in different locations, to strengthen
  their capacities and align their activities with international best
  practices. Trainings were organized in cooperation with local
  institutions such as the Directorate of Nationality, Passports, and
  Immigration.

- In Uganda, IOM conducted several trainings for border officials on
  border management and migration. For instance, in March 2024 for 37
  West Nile border officials and in May 2024 for border officials from
  Uganda’s western regions. These trainings focused on border security,
  trade facilitation, and migration management to learn best practices
  for assisting vulnerable migrants.
